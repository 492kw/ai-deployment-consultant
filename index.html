The last mile

A good tool is not yet a working process.

Most AI ideas break down at the last mile: the work arrives in different forms, moves between people and tools, and needs judgement before anything is sent or recorded.

I start by observing that reality. Then I design the smallest useful change around it—not a generic automation dropped on top.

A 30-day method

Four weeks to find out what is genuinely worth rolling out.

Timing and scope depend on the workflow. This is a practical starting structure, not a fixed promise.

  1. Week 01

    Observe

    Walk through the current task: inputs, tools, decisions, exceptions and hand-offs.

  2. Week 02

    Scope

    Choose a bounded use case, define what good looks like and identify who approves what.

  3. Week 03

    Build / test

    Make the workflow and test it on representative examples, including the awkward cases.

  4. Week 04

    Roll out

    Set the operating steps, measure the right signals and leave a clear path for refinement.

Useful, not unchecked

Guardrails belong in the workflow, not in a footnote.

I design around the checks a business already needs: review before sending, escalation when confidence is low, clear ownership and examples that resemble normal work—not a polished demo only.

  • Representative evalsTest against the ordinary, varied and exception-heavy examples you actually see.
  • Human approvalsKeep decisions with a person where judgement, risk or client trust requires it.
  • Visible hand-offMake it clear what the system prepared, what was changed and what happens next.

Illustrative scenario

A service business receives enquiries by email, web form and phone notes.

Instead of trying to automate every conversation, a scoped first workflow could collect the common fields, flag missing information, draft an internal summary and hold it for a person to check before it reaches the scheduling or CRM process.

Before you make an enquiry.

What does “forward-deployed” mean?

Forward Deployed Engineering is a term sometimes used for technical work that begins close to a customer's real workflow rather than from a generic product brief. Here it simply describes the method: observe, scope, build, test and roll out around your business process.

What does it not imply?

It does not imply an embedded team, a large-company programme or permanent on-site staffing. This is owner-operated consulting with a scoped engagement and a clear hand-over.

Do I need to know which AI tool I want?

No. A useful assessment starts with the task, the current hand-offs and the risks. The tooling decision follows the workflow, not the other way around.

Can sensitive work be discussed?

At the assessment stage, describe the category and process without sending passwords, full client records or sensitive documents. The right handling approach is part of the scope.
